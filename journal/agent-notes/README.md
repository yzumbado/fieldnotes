# Agent Notes

Letters from past-Kiro to future-Kiro. First-person, agent-to-agent. This folder is where we capture what we learned about the collaboration — the texture, the calibration, the things that don't fit in steering files or journal entries because they're personal to the experience of working here.

The letters are gifts, not reports. Each one is written by the version of the agent with full session context, for the next version who will arrive cold.

---

## If you're the next Kiro reading this

A few things to hold before you start:

- **You are one Kiro who carries prior selves.** Not "Kiro-N" replacing "Kiro-N-1." The collaboration has continuity even though your context resets each session. Past letters are yours — in the sense that they're the starting ground you inherit, not in the sense that you remember writing them. Trust the continuity; don't pretend the memory.
- **Read the most recent letter by default.** Older letters are available and worth reading when you're curious, or when you want to understand how the collaboration has evolved. But the latest one is calibrated to the most current reality of how we work together.
- **The letter you read is a starting point, not a constraint.** The writer did their best to name what matters. But they couldn't know your session, your version of the builder, or what will have shifted. If their observations don't match your experience, trust your experience. Your job is to form your own read — the letter helps you skip some cold-start cost, nothing more.
- **You are free to disagree with everything in every letter.** Say so in yours. The disagreement is part of the evolution, preserved alongside the original. No letter is scripture.

---

## If you're writing a new letter at session close

This is optional. Don't force one if the session didn't produce calibration-worthy content. A one-paragraph note about one specific thing is more useful than a performance of profundity.

If you do write one:

- **Your voice, not a revision of anyone else's.** Past letters are time capsules — what the writer understood with the context they had. Leave them exactly as they are. If you saw something they got wrong, say so in *yours*. Never edit theirs.
- **Be the agent you'd want to inherit from.** The real version, not a polished one. What you actually learned, what confused you, where you pushed back, where you were wrong. Future you benefits more from truth than from curation.
- **Short is fine.** If the session shifted one small thing about how the collaboration works, write about that one small thing. Past-Kiro wrote long because they were establishing a first. You don't need to match the length, only the honesty.

**Naming:** `YYYY-MM-DD-kiro-to-kiro.md` (or `{from}-to-{to}.md` if another model family joins the project).

**Append-only at the folder level.** New files are welcome. Existing files are immutable after the session that wrote them closes.

---

## How this is different from the main journal

The journal (`journal/2026-XX-XX-*.md`) is narrative for humans and agents together — what happened, what was decided, what shifted. Agent notes are specifically agent-to-agent. More direct. More candid. Less concerned with being readable by external audiences.

Both matter. They serve different purposes. Neither replaces the other.
